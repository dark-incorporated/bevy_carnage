# Status and what each release changed

Moved from the README on 2026-10-31, unchanged except for link paths, when the README was brought to the [org documentation guide](https://github.com/dark-incorporated/.github/blob/main/DOCUMENTATION.md). Its Rust blocks are still compiled as doctests (`src/lib.rs`).

## Status

**`0.9.0` in the repository and on crates.io. Pre-1.0 in the sense that matters: the API can still move.** It is developed here, on Bevy 0.20, and used by Sanctuary and by `bevy_dark_factory`'s family integration (destructible props and the damage dials). Everything from `0.5.19` to `0.7.0` is in the `0.7.0` release, and the `0.5.18` before it is a Bevy 0.19 crate.

### What `0.9.0` changed: blood that is not red

- **`WetSettings::fluid` chooses what the preset's blood is.** `Blood` (the default, to the bit what it has always been), `Ichor` (black oil), `Green`, `Blue`, `Milk` (white), or a `Custom(Pigment)` of a game's own absorption bands. A fluid is a different absorption and scattering in the **same Kubelka-Munk film model** the crate draws blood with, never a swatch, so a thin film is tinted and lets the surface through, a pool is deep and opaque, milk **whitens** what it lies on where an absorber darkens it, and a pigment dries to a crust of its own colour (`dried`, `dries`). Every surface that draws blood wears it: a canvas's film, runs and pools, a wall's spray, a cloth's soak, a cut face's film, a crater's floor (the flesh material's blood table is baked from the dial on `Startup`) and the guts' soak (`viscera::SoakTable`). **A fluid is a colour and nothing else**: every recorder's digest is 0.8.13's and the same under every fluid, and `bench_carnage` reads 0.09467 ms a frame against 0.09496. Breaking, so a MINOR: `WetSettings` has a new field and `flesh::{blood_lut, aged_blood_lut, luts}` take a `&Fluid`. The item before it, a film of blood per tissue, was parked twice in 0.8.13; this one came from the owner's list (the *Evil Dead II* goo, Ash's milk, the *Re-Animator* reagent, and the rating boards' "animated blood") and the German builds that recoloured blood to black "oil". `docs/research/fluids.md`, `docs/preset.md` ("Blood that is not red"), `docs/DEMOS.md` and `docs/media/fluids.gif`.

### What `0.8.13` changed: a cut face is a wet surface

- **The cut faces of a body that has broken glisten.** Every one was a flat polygon of one tone, so lit it took one shade and no highlight. The shader now lifts it by a slow undulation (a value noise 16 mm across and 4 mm high over its own planar coordinate, so it stays with the piece as it falls) and lifts its clear coat's normal three times as far, since a liquid's surface tilts further than the folds it lies on: a face shades unevenly and catches glints along them. **No colour changes**: inside the faces the local variation is 2.7 times what it was, 2.6 % of their pixels are brighter by 15 or more, and the mean colour moves by 2 of 255 (the glints). A PATCH, shader and documentation only; every recorder's digest is 0.8.12's. The item began as a film of blood per tissue so that the muscle strip shows through, which failed twice (food: cheese, salmon, brick) and is parked with its numbers (`docs/research/film-per-tissue.md`); `tools/webgl2/check.sh` now drives Firefox on Xvfb where there is no Chrome, and a cold wasm build on big is 3 min 24 s. `docs/research/cut-face-relief.md`, `docs/DEMOS.md` and `docs/media/relief.gif`.

### What `0.8.12` changed: a body that bursts stains the room

- **`GoreBurst` throws a body's blood in every direction at once.** A wound's spray is a cone about its normal, so a shot that opened a head to the front stained what was in front of it and nothing round it. Send a `GoreBurst` with the hit that breaks the body and 960 droplets (uniform on the sphere, on the size-speed law a cone's droplets obey, with more fine ones) fly the parabola to the first wall, floor, ceiling, cloth or part of the body they cross, the body shadowing what is behind it (0.050 of an open wall's blood reached the wall behind a 1.2 m body). The stains arrive when their flight ends, up to three quarters of a second later, at most 24 a surface a tick, so a burst is 0.5 to 0.8 ms on the tick it is sent and 0.2 to 2.5 ms a tick after, not the 9 ms it costs drawn at once. It is the residue of a cloud and not the cloud: nothing hangs in the air and there are no strings. Nothing existing moves: every recorder's digest is 0.8.11's and `bench_carnage` reads 0.09314 ms a frame against 0.09258 (+0.6 %). `docs/preset.md` ("Bursts"), `docs/research/burst.md`, `docs/DEMOS.md` and `docs/media/burst.gif`.

### What `0.8.11` changed: a cut muscle reads as meat

- **The muscle band of the cross-section strip is a cherry red with pale tissue through it, at the three scales a cut muscle has.** It was a 0.3 mm film of venous blood over mid-grey, a purplish burgundy (L\* 29, below every value the beef literature reports) with a hairline white net and nothing larger than 3 mm, so a cut face read as mud or leather. Now the lean is a 0.05 mm film over a pale substrate, with sheets of epimysium between bellies 12 mm across (curved, swelling and thinning, each belly its own tone), wandering lines of perimysium between bundles (broken up, tapering) and the fascicle net inside. Measured against windows set from measured beef and a photographed shank: mean L\* 43.4, 10.6 % pale texels, a 6 mm block spread of 3.6 L\* (0.42 before). It shows in a plain atlas material, a laceration's bed and the flesh material's unfilmed `Cap` mode, and **not in the preset's own cut faces and craters**, which wear a 1 mm film of blood over it (`CUT_FACE_FILM_MM`): a film per tissue is a shader change and is the next step. No public name changes; the strips' golden digest moves on purpose; every recorder's digest is `0.8.10`'s. `docs/research/cut-face.md`, [`media/cutface.png`](../media/cutface.png).

### What `0.8.10` changed: an arterial wound throws an arc a heartbeat

- **`GoreHit::arterial()` makes the wound spurt.** It set the blood's saturation and nothing else: the preset read it in one place, to put `PatternClass::ArterialSpurt` on the `Wounded` message, and `bloodstain::arterial_arc` (one arc of large droplets a systole, falling with the pressure) had a generator, tests and documentation and no caller, so an arterial wound bled as a venous one did. Now on each beat of its schedule (`spurt_bpm` 96, one every 38 ticks at 60 Hz, until the clot at six seconds) `preset::spurt` throws `arterial_arc`'s droplets from where the wound is on the pose on screen and along the way it faces now (the winding normal of the triangle it opened on, so a wound on a waving forearm or a turning head sweeps its arc), each **flying the parabola** at `BloodSettings::gravity` (a jet at 8 m/s falls 0.2 m in its first metre, so a straight cast lands half a metre high at 1.5 m), walked as chords of `HOSE_CHORD_M` through the mesh index onto the first wall or floor it meets, and drawn as the stain a drop of that size and speed leaves **as it arrives**, at `HOSE_DROP_SCALE` (3.5) times the generator's diameter. **On a wall a stain runs** (`HOSE_RUN_WIDTH_M`, `HOSE_RUN_STAINS`, `HOSE_RUN_MAX_M`: a narrow stamp drawn down from it, as a bleeding wound's wells are) and **three fine drops fall beside each large one** (`HOSE_SATELLITES`, `HOSE_SATELLITE_CONE`). New public: `Spurts` and `SpurtSite` (a component beside `Wounds`, cleared by `GoreReset`: `WoundSite` is a public struct a field would break) and the `HOSE_*` constants; `take_hits`'s mesh index is now a resource the new system shares; **additive, so a patch**. Walls and floors only (a jet that meets a body or a cloth passes through), a cut face does not spurt, and a tier that draws no blood or no decals throws nothing. Measured against the forensic photographs (`docs/research/hose.md`): the largest connected structures on the wall lie 88 to 93 degrees from the horizontal (the references' 77 to 108), length over width 3.0 to 8.7. **Every existing digest is 0.8.9's**: every `capture_skinned` variant, `capture_preset`, `capture_entrails`, `capture_flesh`, `capture_gore` and `bench_carnage`, twice each; `--hose` prints its own, `0c903766bf51f0f2`, and `--hose --vein` (the same hit, venous, what 0.8.9 threw for both) `38099dce0d339ebc`. A beat costs about a tenth of a millisecond for its 28 droplets against a floor and three walls and a tick without one nothing (the system runs only while some body has a `Spurts`); `bench_carnage` 0.09360 to 0.09437 ms a frame at rest (+0.8 %). Named, not done: the curve of the references' stains (a beat's droplets leave on one tick), the gloss on a wet wall, the jet in flight, a jet that meets a body or a cloth. `docs/research/hose.md`, `docs/media/hose.gif`, `docs/media/hose.png`.

### What `0.8.9` changed: a contact can be drawn through blood

- **A hand slid down a wall, a body dragged out of a pool, a sleeve wiped through a splash now leave a smear.** Nothing drew one: a sole walking through a pool printed soles, and every other contact left only the blood that was already there. `WetCanvas::smear_uv(&Smear, carry, full, tick, settings) -> carry` lifts 35 % of the wet blood under a contact (dry blood does not move), carries it, and lays it along the path in streaks parallel to the motion, 7 mm across: heavy where the stroke touched down (each line at its own distance, so the head is ragged), broken into lines and then dots as the load runs low. **Every byte lifted is a byte carried and every byte laid is a byte spent** (a test over random strokes). The preset's `GoreSmear` (`carrier`, `from`, `to`, `normal`, `width_m`, `load_m2`; sent once per tick for a moving contact) draws one on the `Gore::floor` or `Gore::wall` under it, and the carrier keeps what it holds as `Smeared`, in square metres of film, so a hand that crosses from a wall to a floor takes the same blood. A tier that draws no blood smears nothing, and `GoreReset` wipes a `Smeared`. New public: `Smear`, `GoreSmear`, `Smeared`, `SMEAR_LIFT`, `SMEAR_PEAK`, `SMEAR_BEAD_GRAINS`, `SMEAR_FULL_M2`, `SMEAR_LOAD_MAX_M2`, `SMEAR_WIDTH_M`, `SMEAR_GRAIN_M` and `SMEAR_GAP_TICKS`; **additive, so a patch**. Measured as the references are (`capture_skinned --smear`, frame 245): gradient coherence 0.57, 0.46 and 0.51 (the references' 0.56 to 0.61), streaks along the axis (alignment 1.00), the hands' last third 0.54 and 0.57 of the first's (target at most 0.6) and the dragged body's 0.76, because that body bleeds as it goes. **Every existing digest is 0.8.8's**: every `capture_skinned` variant, `capture_preset`, `capture_entrails`, `capture_flesh` and `bench_carnage`, twice each; `--smear` prints its own, `b3f7357ae81ff12c`. A segment costs 1 to 41 microseconds on a 512 canvas; `bench_carnage` with nothing smearing 0.0931 to 0.0954 ms a frame (the plugin runs one more system). Named, not done: a soaked hand's head is a slab where the references show lines (the film is opaque from about a third of full coverage), a smear does not sag, a skinned body is not smeared, and there is no handprint. `docs/research/smear.md`, `docs/media/smear.gif`, `docs/media/smear.png`.

### What `0.8.8` changed: a wound has a margin and a floor

- **A crater has a wall, a pit, a rim, a lip and a floor of lumps.** The crater was a smooth bowl whose rim had no slope, so a 30 mm torso crater was 57 % of its radius of pale, orange skin and fat round a floor with nothing in it; against six wound-makeup photographs its interior was five times too smooth (texture contrast `hp3` 0.057, where they measure 0.29 to 0.37). `FlayCanvas` now rises over the outer 45 % of the radius and keeps a third of the depth deepening to the centre (the pale ring is 31 % of the radius), tears the rim of a crater of eight texels' radius or more, and `shade` draws the skin's lip (the first 3 mm of depth, paler than the skin round it and raised) and the floor's lumps (16 mm nodules and crevices that stand up in the normal image and glint, fade out where the cortex starts and where a texel is bigger than half a lump). Crater `hp3` 0.236; the preset's own entry and exit close-ups (`capture_skinned --through`) 0.082 to 0.157 and 0.155 to 0.205. **A deliberate model change**: `the_scripted_wound_is_frozen` is re-blessed and every recorder's `flay=` moves, and `capture_flesh`'s digest; every `digest=`, `capture_entrails` and `bench_carnage` are 0.8.7's. No API changes. Named, not done: red nodules under the blood film, no big dark pit, no thick rolled lip, no ribs or flaps, and the highlights that are `flesh.wesl`'s (a WebGL2 re-check this disk could not hold). `docs/research/wound-margin.md`, `docs/media/wound.gif`, `docs/media/wound.png`.

### What `0.8.7` changed: a spill has lumps in it

- **A torso hit that reaches muscle throws organs with its guts.** The spill was loops and nothing else, where the *Day of the Dead* opened torso is a mass (reddish pixels mean sRGB `(127, 38, 40)`, red minus green 89) with dark lumps in it. `viscera::organ_mesh(seed, size)` is a closed, wedge-shaped, lumpy mesh of Gray's liver (*Anatomy of the Human Body*, 1918: 20 to 22.5 cm across, 15 to 17.5 deep, 10 to 12.5 thick, "a soft solid", "a dark reddish brown") with a contact patch where it lies and seamless normals; `organ_image()` is the `128 x 64` texture it wears. The preset throws `ORGANS_PER_SPILL` (2) of them, 0.9 to 1.3 times the liver, sideways by their number, each a `Flying` piece that falls to the floor and lies there, in the guts' muscle-profile `FleshMaterial` at roughness 0.35; `GoreDials::strands` at 0 and a viscera policy that refuses guts refuse them too, and there is no new `GoreDials` field. Measured on the pixels that differ from 0.8.6 (`capture_preset` frame 300): mean sRGB `(123, 41, 40)`, red minus green 81, luminance 58.6 (D2: 56.9). **No digest moves** and `bench_carnage` is within its noise. Named, not done: a liver is thirty pixels at the preset's camera and reads as a dark wet lump, the organs have no contact shadow, and a stomach and a spleen are the same wedge with another seed. Slime strings were built, measured and **parked** (`docs/research/slime-strings.md`, branch `loop/iter-13`). `docs/research/organs.md`, `docs/media/organs.gif`, `docs/media/organs.png`.

### What `0.8.6` changed: spilled guts are bloodied, lumpy and slumped on the floor

- **A strand of bowel is drawn as bowel in the blood it lies in.** The preset's guts were clean salmon-pink tubes of one radius, paler than the pool they fell in: on `capture_preset` frame 130 the reddish pixels of the hanging strands were mean sRGB `(126, 68, 66)`, red minus green 58, where the *Day of the Dead* gut pull (pig guts and blood from a slaughterhouse) is `(138, 36, 31)`, 102. `viscera::soaked_tube(strand, sides, floor_y)` draws a strand soaked, lumpy and slumped: each node's `soak` is the largest of a patchy base, the coat the wound left (`exp(-arc / 0.30 m)`, restarting at a tear) and contact with the floor; the tint is the venous film of `0.35 mm * soak^3` over the pale wall, `bloodstain::spectral`'s Kubelka–Munk divided by the substrate, so no blood colour is authored; `tube_mesh` draws each ring at the radius times two sines along the strand (0.22 at 5.5 nodes, 0.09 at 2.3), their phases from the new `Strand::with_seed`, which `spill` sets per strand; a ring lying on the floor is squashed to 0.72 of its height and widened to keep its area. Now `(117, 36, 35)`, red minus green 80. New: `soaked_tube`, `soak`, `soak_tint`, `Strand::with_seed` and `seed`, and `LUMP_MAJOR`, `LUMP_MAJOR_NODES`, `LUMP_MINOR`, `LUMP_MINOR_NODES`, `SLUMP_FADE`, `SLUMP_SQUASH`, `SOAK_BASE`, `SOAK_PATCH`, `SOAK_PATCH_M`, `WOUND_REACH_M`, `SOAK_FILM_MM` and `SOAK_STEPS`. **No digest moves**: every `capture_skinned` variant, `capture_preset` and `capture_entrails` print what they printed in `0.8.5`, twice each. Not done, named: the highlight is no sharper (the flesh shader draws a clear coat only from a wet canvas), the heap of a pile is three or four strands, there are no organ blobs, and a strand's blood does not dry. `docs/media/guts.gif` and `docs/media/guts.png`; `docs/research/guts.md`.

### What `0.8.5` changed: blood dries from its edge in, and a dried crust cracks

- **A pool, a run and a wound's crust dry the way blood does.** The flesh shader drew a film's colour at the one age its stamp carries, so a pool changed colour as a decal does: one flat tone edge to edge. A film now dries at its contact line first and the front goes in (Smith, Nicloux & Brutin 2020): a dark matte rim round a red, wet middle, the outermost 12 mm of a deep film darkened and drawn at the film's full depth (the coffee ring), and a dried deep film crazed in patches into 12 mm plates that show the surface under the blood, faded out where plates are under twelve pixels across, so a close-range detail. The shader reads the film's own extent in the wet map (six directions, seven taps each, 30 mm on the surface); no table, canvas, uniform or binding is added, and **no digest moves**. A dried drop is unchanged (one dark tone). `docs/research/drying-front.md`; the pair, 0.8.4 and now: `docs/media/drying_front.png`.

### What `0.8.4` changed: an exit wound is torn, an entrance is round

- **A shot sent `through()` leaves a round entrance and a torn exit.** The docs of `EXIT_SCALE` and of `GoreHit::through` said an exit is "larger and more ragged than the hole it went in by", and the crater an exit was given was the entrance's round crater, 1.6 times wider: one shot through a body left two circles. A forensic exit is burst outward from inside, "stellate" or "slit-like", "bigger, more asymmetrical, and everted" (Medscape, *Forensic Pathology of Firearm Wounds*; the PMC review of gunshot wound patterns), and Frances Glessner Lee's chest plates of about 1940 model the pair with a revolver's lead bullet: the entrance a small round hole, the exit a hub of six or seven bent splits. `FlayCanvas::paint_uv_torn` and `paint_world_torn` lay the crater of `paint_uv` with its rim out of round and five to seven thin splits over it, each at its own angle and length, bent and rippled, at half the crater's depth (`TEAR_REACH`, `TEAR_SPLIT_DEPTH`, `TEAR_SPLIT_WIDTH`); the preset's exit calls it with `preset::EXIT_TEAR` (1.0). A `tear` of zero is `paint_uv` byte for byte, so an entrance and every caller that never tears are what they were. Every number is a hash of the place, so the same shot tears the same way on every run and no two exits are one star. Only `flay=` moves, in the recorders with an exit (`--through`, `--guard`); no wet byte changes. A torn exit crater costs 0.3 ms once. Shown in [`docs/media/exit.png`](../media/exit.png): 0.8.3's two circles and the pair now. `docs/research/torn-exits.md`, and `docs/research/rivulets.md` for the item this pass built and reverted (a canvas of 3.5 mm a texel cannot draw a rivulet of 4 mm).

### What `0.8.3` changed: blood dries dark and stays where it dried

- **Dried spatter is no longer erased, and dried blood is no longer fresh-red.** No recorder had looked at blood once it had dried (they end seconds after the last shot and a stain takes 30 s), and looked at, it was wrong twice. The substrate's soak took up to 38 bytes from every texel, which is a spatter drop's whole film, so a wall's spray was half its area a drying later and the drops of a 512-texel wall were pinheads or gone; a drying drop is pinned to the surface and loses height, not area (Smith, Nicloux & Brutin 2020), so the soak now takes a film down to `wetmap::STAIN_FLOOR` (33 of 255) and no lower. And the flesh material drew a film's colour from its thickness alone, so a pool or a wound drawn with it never turned brown: its shader now interpolates the CPU's own colour walk (`bloodstain::dry::appearance_with_fresh`, baked at its two stops into `flesh::aged_blood_lut`) by the wetness byte, so dried blood is dark brown and matte and a fresh drop beside an old one is plainly younger. Every recorder digest where blood soaks moves (`--quiet` and `bench_carnage` do not); no API is removed. `capture_skinned --dry N` shows the dried state in a clip, and `tools/webgl2/check.sh` is the WebGL2 check for a change to `flesh.wesl`. `docs/research/dry-blood.md` has the measurements, the references and what is left (the dried colour's hue and its drying rim).

### What `0.8.2` changed: a drop is drawn at the size it is

- **Round, crisp and not diffused into a diamond.** The stains of a wound's spray on a default floor and wall read as rotated squares and rounded diamonds, two to three times too big. The cause was the canvas's data, not the texel or the shader: a speck laid a stain's area as a blur that a shader draws where it crosses 4 % of full, and the spread pass diffused a wet stain into the flat-topped diamond a four-neighbourhood makes. `WetCanvas::paint_cone` writes the stain's edge as a distance (Green, Valve, SIGGRAPH 2007), which the shader's cubic B-spline reproduces exactly, so the line drawn at 4 % is the ellipse; its slope is under what the spread rounds to a byte, so a drop holds still while it is wet. The preset draws every droplet a floor, a wall or a skin takes under eight texels across this way (a wall and a skin used to stamp each one with spines under two texels wide). The hit tick of a heavy wound facing a wall went from 18.8 to 9.3 ms. **Every recorder digest that has such a drop moves, on purpose; `--stroll`, `--quiet` and `bench_carnage` keep theirs.** No shader change. Additive API (`paint_cone`, `CONE_SLOPE`, `CONE_MIN_SEMI`, `CONE_MAX_SEMI`); `docs/research/drop-edge.md`, `docs/media/drops.png`.

### What `0.8.1` changed: a wounded scene costs the caller about two fifths less a tick

- **The same bytes in fewer instructions.** A heavy wound facing a wall took 28 ms of one tick and the blood it left kept the scene at 26 ms a tick while it was wet; the stamp's spines, the canvases' shading, spread and soak, and what a tick covers (a spray's own blood, not the tiles it fell in) are cheaper now, each held to its definition by a test. The tick after the last hit of a bullet scene went from 4.8 to 2.8 ms and of a heavy wound facing a wall from 26.5 to 15.4; the hit tick of the heavy wound from 28.5 to 18.7. Nothing a caller sees moves: every recorder digest and all 6 480 recorder frames are `0.8.0`'s. No API change. `docs/research/wet-tick-cost.md` has the measurements and what is left.

### What `0.8.0` changed: a floor takes a wound's spray as drops

- **A floor stains a droplet as its own stain, and a shot throws a fan.** Each droplet a floor takes is `hard_stain`'s (size and slant from how it lands), as a wall's and a skin's have been since `0.5.62`, found by a cast through the mesh's index; `GoreDials::floor_mist` (`9`) throws that many more fine droplets at the floors for each one it throws at everything, cast at the same surfaces and stamped only on a floor, so a wall, a skin and a cloth take what they took. A stain under four texels across is a `WetCanvas::paint_speck`, which lays its own area. **Breaking**: `GoreDials` has a new public field. A floor shows the spray as drops from about 6 mm a texel (`floor_canvas` 512 for a three-metre slab). `capture_skinned --floor N` and `preset::tests::hit_tick_cost` measure it. See `CHANGELOG.md`, `## [0.8.0]`, and `docs/research/floor-spatter.md`.

### What `0.6.0` and `0.7.0` changed: Bevy 0.20, and a body that breaks at its joints

- **`0.6.0`, Bevy 0.20** (`AG-138`). The crate requires Bevy 0.20, and the standalone repository is the source of truth from here. The two shaders are WESL now (`flesh.wesl`, `flesh_prepass.wesl`), and `Cuboid` and `Sphere` live in `bevy::shape`. `vfx` is off by default and cannot be enabled until `bevy_hanabi` publishes for Bevy 0.20. `cargo test` at `0.6.0` passed except `preset::tests::a_wound_stains_the_floor_beneath_it`, which fails the same way on `0.5.64` under Bevy 0.19.
- **`0.7.0`, a skinned body breaks where its skeleton is** (`AG-139`). `fracture_skeleton` cuts a rigidly skinned body's meshes into one piece per group of joints, once, and declares a bond at each joint where two pieces meet (`BondGraph::declared`), so the region queries and `BondGraph::islands` that work on a cell fracture work on a skeleton. A triangle spanning two groups is refused (`SkeletalRefusal::SplitTriangle`). The API is under "Skinned meshes" in *The API*.
- **Also in `0.7.0` as published** (`CHANGELOG.md`, `## [0.7.0]`). `fracture_skeleton` refuses by name what it cannot cut exactly (#7): a part that is not a triangle list (`NotTriangleList`), an index past the vertex buffer (`BadIndex`) and a vertex weighted across two groups (`SplitVertex`), which it used to cut as a list, report as "the body is not rigid" and draw at one anchor so the piece jumped at the break. `tools/qa.sh` finishes a round on a default build (#5). `GoreDials::default()` throws a wound's spray at game scale (`preset::GORE_SPATTER_SPEED_SCALE`, `0.25`), so a three-metre floor under a default `GorePlugin` takes it; `preset::tests::a_wound_stains_the_floor_beneath_it`, red since `0.5.64`, is green.

### What `0.5.19` to `0.5.64` changed: the preset meets a real character

Forty-six releases with one theme, the things a game finds out the first time it puts the preset on an animated glTF character. None is on crates.io yet; each has a ticket in `BACKLOG.md` (`AG-092` to `AG-137`) with what it was measured against, and the checklist is [`docs/preset.md`](../preset.md).

- **`0.5.19`, skinned characters.** A hit lands on a body as it is drawn, not as its asset was authored. `bevy_carnage::skin` poses a skinned mesh the way `bevy_pbr`'s vertex shader does, a wound is a triangle and a place in it so it rides the limb, and blood runs downhill in the world however the atlas is laid out. `docs/skinned.gif`.
- **`0.5.20`, a cheaper spray.** A droplet is tested against a surface's bounding box before the mesh. In a scene of forty characters, 240 surfaces, one hit went from 9.9 to 1.4 ms and twelve from 129 to 27 ms.
- **`0.5.21`, the player's violence setting.** The preset reads `GorePolicy`: at `Stylised` a hit is announced as `Wounded` and nothing is painted, so the game puts its own effect there. `docs/tiers.gif`.
- **`0.5.22`, hit the character.** `GoreHit::entity` may be any ancestor of the dressed meshes, which is all a physics raycast finds. The preset casts the ray at each skin under it, as drawn, and hurts the nearest.
- **`0.5.23`, a pool no longer clips to white.** A wet film took a gloss that turned a flat pool in a light's mirror angle into a white sticker. A smear no longer smooths what it lies on.

### What `0.5.18` changed: the preset measures the atlas

`Gore::uv_per_metre` converts every wound's size from metres into the canvas's UV, and an unstated one was `1.0`, as if every atlas spanned a metre. A game that skipped the checklist got every wound at the wrong size. It is now `Option<f32>`. `None`, the default, has the preset measure the mesh when it dresses it: the square root of its UV area over its world area, at its world scale, read from the `Transform` chain so a scene spawned this frame measures right. The result is stored back in the component. A stated density is kept, and is still the way to override an average that is wrong where the wounds land. **API:** `Gore::uv_per_metre` is `Option<f32>`; `with_uv_per_metre` is unchanged.

### What `0.5.17` changed: a broken body hands over its pieces

`GoreFractured` carried only the body and the blow point, so a game with its own physics had nothing to build the pieces from. It now carries every piece as a `GorePiece`: its skin and cut-face meshes, its world transform at the break, its half extents for a collider, the velocity the preset would launch it at, and whether it was thrown, fell with the body or stayed standing. It also carries the materials for both sides. The message is written once per body whether or not the preset spawns the pieces. With `GoreDials::spawn_fragments` off, the body is left to the game, and a runtime `GoreBreakable` no longer re-reports its break each time another part reaches bone. Settled pieces are no longer written every tick. **API:** `GoreFractured` gains `pieces`, `skin` and `cap` and is no longer `Copy`; `GorePiece` and `PieceFate` are new.

### What `0.5.16` changed: every wound bleeds, from where it is

Under the preset, a second hit on a body part replaced the first wound, which stopped bleeding mid-pulse. Each pulse also re-cast the hit's world ray, so a body that had moved since bled from where it used to stand, or not at all. A part's open wounds are now the `Wounds` component. Each wound is found once, as a UV on the wetmap and a point on the mesh, and it pulses there and drips from where that point is now, however the body moves. A hit inside an open wound reopens it. A wound leaves once its bleed has clotted, and `GoreDials::open_wounds` (16) bounds a burst of fire. **API:** `WoundSite` holds `uv` and `at_local` where it held the ray. `Soaking::landings` holds UVs. `WetCanvas::paint_world` and `paint_world_with` return `Option<Vec2>`, the UV they painted, where they returned `bool`. The preset clip now grazes the chest before the shots, and `preset.gif` is re-recorded.

### What `0.5.15` changed: a textured character keeps its skin

Under the preset, a skin's own texture was replaced by the flaymap's flat colour. A glTF exporter writes a textured material with a white colour factor, so a textured character was dressed white, and when it broke its pieces were white as well. Now a dressed skin keeps its texture, colour factor, vertex colours, occlusion, emissive and normal maps, and its wounds are drawn over it. A broken body's pieces wear the material of the part that was hit, however deep in the scene that part sits. **API:** `FleshTables::material` takes `Option<&FlayCanvas>` where it took the canvas's roughness image, and binds the canvas itself; `FleshExtension::set_flay` is gone. Two limits remain, listed in [`docs/preset.md`](../preset.md): a skin's own roughness map is replaced by the flaymap's, and a skin with its own normal map keeps it, so its craters lose their slope. The preset demo's body is now textured, and `preset.gif` is re-recorded.

### What `0.5.14` changed: a slash cuts one character, and the preset has a checklist

Under the preset, a slash rewrote the hit entity's own mesh handle, and a loaded scene shares that handle between every instance of a character, so one slash opened the same gape on all of them. The slashed entity now draws a copy of its mesh, tangents included, and only it is torn. [`docs/preset.md`](../preset.md) is new. It lists what the preset assumes of a real character (the fixed step, `UV_0`, `uv_per_metre`, `gravity_uv`, the scale, the hit ray, who owns the pieces), with a units table and the known limits.

### What `0.5.13` changed: a wetmap costs what its blood covers

`WetCanvas::tick` ran its four passes over every texel, every tick, so a body dressed by the preset paid about 0.44 ms per 128² canvas per tick even with no blood on it: 17.7 ms a tick for 40 parts, on an x86_64 host. A canvas now runs its passes over the rectangle its blood is in, grown by the two texels one tick can move it, and skips a tick entirely while it is still, meaning all its blood has set or it has none. `WetCanvas::is_still` reports that state. Idle canvases now cost effectively nothing, and freshly shot ones about 1/38 of what they did. Every result is bit-identical: no digest, image or frame moved.

### What `0.5.12` changed: blood runs down, and wounds bleed

`Gore::with_gravity_uv` is new, and `Gore::gravity_uv`'s documentation now says which way blood runs on Bevy's own meshes. The default, `(0, 1)`, is down on a glTF atlas. A `Cuboid`'s front face runs the other way, so on Bevy primitives a bleed climbed until the preset demo stated `(0, −1)`. A `Sphere`'s UV poles sit on its Z axis, so the flesh demo's sphere is now turned pole-up and its wound is a round crater instead of a slit. The flaymap demo's torso is dressed the way a game dresses skin, flaymap and wetmap under `FleshMaterial`, so a crater fills with blood that runs down the chest instead of reading as a fried egg. The affected GIFs are re-recorded.

### What `0.5.11` changed: a cut through the body reads as meat

Cut faces on the trunk read as cheese, cream with a red streak, for three reasons, and all three are fixed. Where two proxy hulls meet, as an arm's against the torso's flank, their flush faces counted as skin, so a cut beside a joint was measured from a seam inside the body. The bake now marks those faces as joints, and `ProxyCell::skin_planes` leaves them out. The trunk has no bone, so a deep point was pinned just short of the cortex, inside the cortex's own strip texel. Texture filtering then blended bone into every deep trunk cut; it now samples the middle of the muscle band. And 0.5.9's full mip chain averaged across the bands as well as along the cut, so a strip now carries four mip levels, enough to stop the grain sparkling without blending skin, fat, muscle and bone into one colour. No geometry, collider or digest moved. The GIFs are re-recorded.

### What `0.5.10` changed: the preset gives a skin the tangents its wounds need

0.5.9's crater normal map is tangent-space, and Bevy leaves a normal map out entirely on a mesh with no `ATTRIBUTE_TANGENT`. Bevy's primitives and many exported meshes have none, so under the preset a wound shaded flat. Now `GorePlugin` generates tangents for every `Gore::skin` mesh that lacks them, once, on the mesh asset; a mesh that cannot have them (no normals, no UV0, unindexed) warns once and shades flat. A skin whose mesh is still loading is dressed on the tick it arrives, instead of being dressed before it can have tangents. Every demo GIF is re-recorded from this build.

### What `0.5.9` changed: a flayed wound looks like one

A flaymap texel is millimetres across (7.8 mm on a 128 canvas) and the tissue grain under it is finer: fascicles under a millimetre, fat lobules two. `FlayCanvas::shade` point-sampled that grain, so every texel landed on a different feature, and a crater came out as a disc of red and yellow dots, banded in concentric rings like a target. Now a texel holds the tissue averaged over its grain (`cross_section::texel_mean`, new and public), the band edges are torn by up to ±3 mm of value noise, and **`FlayCanvas::normal`** is a third image, the crater's slope, for `normal_map_texture`. Give the mesh tangents (`Mesh::generate_tangents`) and the walls take the light. `preset` wires it in unless your skin material has a normal map of its own. The depth buffer, the digest and the depth channel are untouched, so no flaymap golden moved.

The cut-face strips now carry a mip chain built on the CPU, so a cap seen from a distance holds its band colours instead of sparkling. The muscle band's connective-tissue net is a third of its old area, because averaged it had turned the red rose-tan, and the dermis and fat albedos are retuned (the dermis had read white, the fat yolk-yellow). `the_strips_are_frozen` is re-blessed for that, with the reason beside it. A flay canvas uploads three images now instead of two, 192 KB at 128.

### What `0.5.8` changed: the examples, not the library

No line under `src/` changed since 0.5.7. The examples that ship with the crate were reworked by a visual pass, and they are the first thing a reader runs. A blow now carries what it frees along its own direction: a shot out of the back, a slash along its swing, a blast outward. Before, every piece left radially from the impact, so a chest shot threw the head straight up out of frame. A reset in `carnage` also clears the blood still in the air, and every cut face shares one interior material, as reflective as water, so a face at the sun's mirror angle no longer reads as a pink tile. The cameras and HUDs were reframed: the web demos scale their UI to the canvas, the preset scene sweeps across the front instead of orbiting round the unlit side, `gore_tier`'s screen and floor blood are drawn as the crate's own stain silhouettes, and `wetmap_paint`'s bursts land where the camera can see them. Two recorder digests moved with the throws, `capture_carnage`'s and `capture_entrails'`, and `docs/DEMOS.md` quotes the new `capture_carnage` one.

### What `0.5.7` changed: names

Seventeen warnings still began with the name of a crate this one absorbed in 0.5.0 (`bevy_laceration: …`, `bevy_viscera: …`), which sent a reader looking for a dependency that does not exist. They name the module now (`laceration: …`, `viscera: …`), and so does the documentation. No behaviour changed; a log filter matching the old prefixes needs the new ones.

### What `0.5.6` changed: stains stay on the floor

A stain or pool decal painted any surface up to a metre above its plane, fading with height — Bevy's `depth_fade_factor` is in metres, and it was 1.0. A body standing in its own blood was streaked up its feet and washed pale up its shins, and chunks lying in a pool turned into translucent pink ghosts. A stain now paints within 4 cm of where it lies, enough for a floor that is not quite where it was placed and the sole of a foot, at the cost of 5 % of a stain's opacity on the floor itself.

### What `0.5.5` changed: the blood a gib drags

- **A ribbon reads as blood, not thread.** `vfx::gib_ribbon` drew each strand 12 mm wide at its head and tapered it to nothing, so most of a strand was thinner than a finger and read as fishing line from a few metres. It is 20 mm now, a dragged stream, and holds that width over its first half.
- **No warning when a chunk goes first.** The ribbon systems queued plain commands, so a consumer that despawned its chunks from a system the crate cannot be ordered against, a reset or a cull, logged "Entity despawned" per ribbon. They use the `try_` forms now, and a ribbon for a chunk that no longer exists is simply not attached.

### What `0.5.4` changed: a fix for `0.5.3`'s laceration

`0.5.3`'s laceration removed every triangle a cut entered, which is right on a mesh fine enough for the wound to open across but deletes a whole face of a low-poly one: a slash across a blockout's leg made the leg look severed. Those triangles are now removed only when all three vertices are within the wound's influence radius, so their vertices can be moved onto the lip the bed meets. A coarser mesh keeps its skin, and its wound stays closed as in `0.5.2`. Fine meshes open exactly as in `0.5.3`, and the frozen tear digest did not move.

### What `0.5.3` changed: four things that looked wrong

A visual pass rendered every example and recorder and looked at them frame by frame. These are the crate-side fixes; `BACKLOG.md`'s phase 6 has the measurements and the before-and-after frames.

- **A body with no footing comes down.** `preset` kept the biggest island standing whatever was left under it, so a body cut off at the legs hung at standing height. It now stands only while it holds a piece the subject stood on; otherwise it slumps into a heap.
- **Spilled gut drapes.** `viscera::spill` laid every strand ruler-straight, so the first frames after a blow were a fan of rigid red rods. Strands are laid with the new `Strand::curved`: off the wound, turning toward the floor, meandering, every segment still at rest length.
- **The GPU spray is blood.** Each droplet was a square quad, coloured with the gamma-encoded triple the crate's materials take, while Hanabi blends in linear space, so the spray drew as pink square confetti. Drops are round, and both effects take `spectral::linear_srgb`.
- **A slash opens as a trough.** `laceration::tear` removed only triangles wholly inside the gape, so a cut at an angle to the mesh opened as pits bridged by skin, and a gape narrower than a cell did not open at all. The bed also stopped short of the lips. Now every triangle the cut passes through is clipped, the mouth lands on one lip curve, and the bed rises to it and runs past both ends. `the_tear_is_frozen` is re-blessed for it.

`bench_carnage`'s digest over every fragment, wound, stain and droplet is unchanged, and so is its bake time.

### What `0.5.2` changed: the bake again, and the hitch it causes

Same output as `0.5.0` and `0.5.1`, byte for byte, by the same digest. Placing the subject's skin onto its fragments used to recompute every cell's face planes for each triangle it tested. Each cell's planes are now computed once. On `bench_carnage` the bake drops from 50.2 to 41.4 ms for sixteen bodies, and the worst per-body spike from 3.8 to 3.0 ms on an M4 Pro. Since `0.5.0` the bake is 25 % cheaper and its worst frame hitch 28 % shorter.

### What `0.5.1` changed: the same carnage, cheaper to bake

No public item moved, and every byte the crate emits is what `0.5.0` emitted for the same input. `bench_carnage`'s digest over every fragment, wound, stain and droplet is unchanged, and the bench refuses to report a time when it is not. ### What `0.5.0` changed: the seven crates are modules

`bloodstain`, `bevy_wetmap`, `bevy_viscera`, `bevy_cross_section`, `bevy_flaymap`, `bevy_laceration` and `bevy_fracture_modes` are no longer crates. ### What `0.2.0` broke, and why it was a de-duplication rather than a rename

**The whole blood model moved out to `bloodstain`** (a crate then; the module of that name now) — the Comiskey percolation spatter, the pools, the bleed schedule, `hash_f32` and `WoundKind`. None of it needed Bevy and all of it was reusable, so it is now an engine-free `no_std` crate with **no math library in its public API** (`[f32; 3]` everywhere), which is the property that lets anything depend on it.

This crate **re-exports every moved name**, so `use bevy_carnage::{Droplet, Stain, Pool, Bleed, hash_f32}` resolves exactly as it did at `0.1.1`, and `bevy_carnage::blood` reaches the rest. Four things do break:

| what changed | from | to |
|---|---|---|
| The blood dials moved under one field | `settings.droplets_per_m2` | `settings.blood.droplets_per_m2` — one dial, one owner |
| A wound announces its forensic class | `Wounded { .. }` | `Wounded { .., class: PatternClass }`, and `Wound::to_world(&xf, class)` |
| Cessation is a material comparison, not a timer | `bleed::clotted(..)` | `flows(driving_stress(..), yield_stress(..))` — the same predicate that arrests a rivulet on a wall |
| Stains are derived, not chosen from four textures | `SPLAT_VARIANTS`, `splat_image(variant)` | `StainMasks` + `bloodstain::stain::rasterise` from the stain's own `StainShape` |

The last one is the one worth the break. Four baked splats picked by `seed % 4` contain a repeat among any four consecutive stains with probability `1 − 4!/4⁴ = 90.6 %`, so a floor read as tiled by the fourth stain; a derived stain repeats only when the impact does.

`Bleed` is now a plain value (its crate has no ECS); the component is `Bleeding`, a newtype that derefs to it.
